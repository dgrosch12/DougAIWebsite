## 6. The Loom-Based Async Sales Process

At the $1,500–$3,000 setup price point, virtually every customer needs a conversation before buying. But it doesn't need to be a 60-minute discovery call. The Loom method enables async selling that works within evening hours.

### 6.1 The Funnel

1. **Prospect Discovery:** Cold email, LinkedIn, or referral drives prospect to landing page
2. **Intake Form:** Prospect fills out a 2-minute form (Tally/Typeform) with business info, pain points, and budget range
3. **Personalized Loom (5–7 min):** Record during evening block — pull up their website, acknowledge by name, walk through visible problems, show where automation connects, give rough ROI estimate
4. **Send Loom + Short Email:** Subject: "Built something for [Business Name]" — include Loom link and Calendly for those who want live conversation
5. **Follow-Up:** 48 hours no response → one follow-up. No response after second email → monthly nurture sequence
6. **Close (Path A — 80%):** Reply to email, answer questions via email or 2-min follow-up Loom, send one-page proposal with Stripe payment link
7. **Close (Path B — 20%):** Book 15–20 min call via Calendly (Wed/Thu 6–8 PM or Sat 9–11 AM only). Call is confirmation, not discovery.

### 6.2 Example Loom Script (Speed-to-Lead for HVAC)

*"Hey [Name], Douglas here. I looked at your website and I can see you've got a form here for requesting a quote. Right now when someone fills this out, it goes to your email inbox. By then that homeowner has already called two other companies. What I'd build is a system that calls you AND the lead within 10 seconds. You pick up, you're instantly connected while they're still on your website. If you're getting 30 leads/month and closing 20%, that's 6 jobs. My clients see that jump to 30–40% because they're first to respond. That's 3–6 extra jobs/month. At $3,000 average ticket, that's $9–$18K in extra revenue. My system costs $2,500 setup and $400/month."*

### 6.3 Why Loom > Live Calls

- Prospect can watch when convenient, rewatch key parts, share with business partner
- Demonstrates technical expertise better than talking on Zoom
- More time-efficient: 5-min Loom takes 5 minutes vs. 30–45 min "quick call" with scheduling overhead
- Actually delivers a better client experience, not worse

### 6.4 Intake Form Fields

## 11. Business Model Progression

### 11.1 Stage 1 (Current — $500 to $5–$10K MRR): Done-for-You

Sell the system, deploy it, client pays setup fee + monthly retainer. Fastest path to revenue. Each client worth $3,000–$8,000 in year one. Only need 10–15 clients to hit income goals. Sales process is one-to-one (Loom + occasional call). Systems already built, just need more clients at better prices.

### 11.2 Stage 2 ($10–$15K MRR): Done-With-You + Templates

After deploying the same Speed-to-Lead system 30+ times and blog automation 5–10 times, package the deployment process into a semi-self-serve product: templated n8n workflow + video walkthrough + setup guide. Price lower ($1,500–$2,500 one-time + $200/month) to capture the market segment that can't afford full done-for-you service.

### 11.3 Stage 3 (5K+ YouTube, 10K+ LinkedIn): Course + Community

The Stephen G Pope model — sell $3–$5K digital products teaching people how to build AI automation systems, or sell templates as licensable products other consultants can resell. This requires an audience that trusts you enough to buy without talking to you, which takes 12–24 months of consistent content to build.

### 11.4 The Stephen G Pope Case Study

Stephen G Pope's Kontent Engine Database ($5K course + Airtable system + community) works because he sold a 7-figure software company, spent years building 74K+ YouTube audience, and has thousands in his ecosystem who already trust him. His "No-Code Architects" Skool community promises "Run a $50K/month 1-person business without sales calls" — but this is for self-serve digital products, not done-for-you services.

**Critical insight:** Douglas is at a different stage — proven systems, real client results, strong technical skills, but not yet the audience, content library, or brand recognition to sell self-serve digital products at volume. Jumping to Stage 3 now means months building a course nobody knows about for a market that doesn't know you, when you could be closing $3,000+ deals with existing systems this month. The play for the next 12 months: sell done-for-you, raise prices, stack recurring revenue, build content presence while serving clients.
